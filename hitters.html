Top 200 Hitters by Stat Above Average

This isn't trying to reproduce the actual Baseball Hall of Fame's membership — it's this project's own answer to who belongs. No linear-weight run values, no replacement-level constant: every player is scored only against the actual league average and spread of their own season, using five genuinely separate things you can point to directly in a box score — batting average, isolated power, walk rate, stolen-base rate, and defensive value. (An earlier version of this list scored OPS as a sixth input alongside isolated power and walk rate — but those two are already sub-ingredients of OPS, so it was quietly counting the same power and patience skills twice. Fixed by scoring average, power, and patience each exactly once.)

What's a Stat Above Average? Each player-season is compared only to that season's own league average and spread — a 1927 season is judged against 1927, a 2021 season against 2021, nothing crosses eras. The five stats above are each converted to a z-score (standard deviations above or below that year's league mean, computed from qualifying hitters only), averaged into one composite for the season, then summed — not averaged — across every qualifying season of a 5,000+ PA career. Summing instead of averaging is deliberate: a short, dominant peak and a long, steady compiling career should both be able to add up to a real Hall of Fame case, not just the peak.

Negro Leagues players qualify on a separate standard, not the 5,000 PA floor. Their official league schedules were far shorter than MLB's (a full Josh Gibson season was around 48 games), and on top of that the surviving record undercounts them further — barnstorming, exhibition, and winter-league games that were a real part of their careers were never entered into any official ledger. A shorter MLB record usually means a genuinely shorter career; a shorter Negro Leagues record often just means a thinner paper trail. So instead, Negro Leagues players qualify by accumulating the equivalent of 9 seasons at their own league's typical season size — the standard that gets Josh Gibson (180th, 9 seasons played) onto this list alongside Willie Wells (90th) and Turkey Stearnes (131st).

Runs scored and RBI were deliberately left out — both depend heavily on who's on base ahead of you and where you hit in the order, not just your own output. The HOF column marks players already inducted into the real Hall of Fame, so you can see at a glance where this list agrees with Cooperstown and where it doesn't.
