The Real Hall of Fame, where talent supersedes opinion.

Ranked on the field, not on ballots.

Welcome to The Real Hall Of Fame, the virtual Hall that includes MLB players who are worthy and deserving of being recognized for their exceptional talents on the baseball diamond. Players are included here based on what they accomplished, illustrated by the statistics they accumulated over the years. We've built a formula that uses irrefutable facts to calculate our RHOF score and only the best of the best are allowed in. There's no five year waiting period after a player retires. There are active players in The Real Hall Of Fame. The players here earned their ticket.

The museum in Cooperstown, NY is a magical place where fans can visit a museum and enjoy an experience like no other. Artifacts of eras and players long past and a gallery of plaques immortalizing those who've been inducted. But there's a real problem with their selection process. They've placed the power to induct or deny admission to their hall into the hands of writers who vote for or against induction based on biased opinions and prejudice. That's a pretty strong statement, so let's explain it.

Hank Aaron was voted into Cooperstown, and deservedly so. But he was not voted in unanimously. He only received 97.8% of the vote. Of the 415 ballots cast, he was named on only 406. Is that right? Absolutely not! 9 writers voted against the induction of the man who had hit the most home runs in baseball history at the time he retired. Willie Mays only received 94.7% of the vote. Ted Williams only received 93.4% of the vote. Really?

Who was the first player to be voted in unanimously? Babe Ruth? Nope. Ty Cobb. Nope. Walter Johnson? Nope. The only player to be voted in unanimously is Mariano Rivera, on the 2019 ballot. Of all the players currently inducted, only 1 has ever received every vote. Only... one.

And why are Bobby Grich (71.1 career WAR) and Lou Whitaker (75.1 career WAR) on the outside looking in while Harold Baines (38.7 career WAR) and Tommy McCarthy (14.7 career WAR) are in?

That just isn't right. So we decided to fix it. That's why we're called The Real Hall Of Fame.

Why not WAR?

Wins Above Replacement measures every player against one universal "replacement level" — a theoretical bench player, calibrated once from decades of injury-substitution data and then applied identically to a dead-ball-era outfielder and a modern closer. It's the industry standard for good reason, but that one number was never derived for either of them specifically.

The RHOF Score measures every player against their own competition instead — the actual league average, in the actual years and at the actual position they played, blended across whichever eras their career spans.

No replacement level. No runs-to-wins conversion borrowed from someone else's research. Just each player's rate stats, held up against the players who were actually on the field around them.
