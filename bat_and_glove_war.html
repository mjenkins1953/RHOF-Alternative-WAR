Bat & Glove WAR

A career value formula built entirely from box-score hitting and fielding lines—no proprietary batted-ball data, no scouting grades—checked against actual career Wins Above Replacement for 1,912 players with 3,000+ plate appearances.

The Formula

Palmer-style linear weights + range & position

Batting Value (runs)

Bat = 0.47×1B + 0.78×2B + 1.09×3B + 1.40×HR + 0.33×(BB+HBP) + 0.225×SB - 0.35×CS - 0.25×(AB−H) + 0.033×PA

Last term restores a flat replacement-level credit (≈20 runs / 600 PA). Stolen-base credit is dialed back to half its fit-optimal value—left at full strength it let raw speed (Rickey Henderson's 1,406 steals) outrank far better all-around players.

Fielding Value (runs)

Field = Range Runs + Position Runs

Range Runs: (chances − league-average chances at that position/year) × 0.5 runs, with career totals shrunk toward zero to tame small-sample noise. Position Runs: a flat credit for how hard the position is to play—independent of how well any one player fielded it.

Position Runs (per 150 defensive games)

What it does and doesn't see

Range Runs come from raw putouts + assists—box-score fielding, not batted-ball tracking—so treat them as a rough signal, not a verdict. Two corrections keep it from going off the rails: a catcher's putouts are mostly strikeouts thrown by the pitcher, not a fielding skill, so catchers get position credit only, no range component; and a first baseman's putouts are mostly just catching throws from teammates, so 1B range is scored on assists alone.

InnOuts (defensive innings) is missing outright for 1911–1953 in this data—essentially the Ruth-through-Musial era—so games played × 9 stands in for innings there. Combined with heavy shrinkage on career totals, that keeps a 20-year Hall-of-Fame career from compounding noise into an implausible number.

Designated hitters needed their own fix: DH isn't a fielding position, so it has no rows in the fielding data at all—David Ortiz's 2,130 career games at DH were invisible to the model until games batted minus games actually fielded was used to back into DH time and apply its (very negative) position credit.

Method. Hitting and fielding totals aggregated from the Chadwick Bureau/Lahman Batting.csv and Fielding.csv career databases (all seasons through the most recent). "Real WAR" is career WAR summed from Baseball-Reference's published war_daily_bat.txt component file, plus pitching WAR for two-way players (e.g. Ruth), to match the public career leaderboard. Players classified as primarily pitchers are excluded, matching the "position player" leaderboard convention.

This is one formula among many possible variants—coefficients, shrinkage, and positional-adjustment values all shift the result at the margins. The point isn't precision; it's how far you can get toward reproducing WAR's shape using nothing but a stat sheet and arithmetic. Adding fielding moved rank agreement from 0.826 (bat only) to 0.883—the single biggest jump of any change made to this formula.
