Top 200 Pitchers by Stat Above Average

The mound version of the hitters list. No borrowed run values, no replacement level: every pitcher is scored only against the actual league average and spread of their own season, on five things straight off the back of a baseball card — ERA, WHIP, strikeout rate, winning percentage, and saves.

What's a Stat Above Average? Each pitcher-season is compared only to that season's own league average and spread — a 1913 season is judged against 1913, a 2024 season against 2024. The five stats are each converted to a z-score, then blended with the weights from this site's RHOF Score — 25% ERA, 25% WHIP, 20% strikeout rate, 20% winning percentage, 10% saves — into one number for the season. (ERA and WHIP are flipped, so a lower-than-league mark is a positive z.) Those season numbers are then summed across every qualifying season, each scaled to a 200-inning workload, so a long steady career and a short dominant one can both add up to a genuine case.

The five aren't weighted equally — the hitters list does that; this one follows the RHOF formula. ERA and WHIP carry half the score between them because preventing runs and baserunners is the job. Strikeout rate is the one bat-missing skill on every stat sheet. Winning percentage and saves round it out at the lowest weights.

Winning percentage and saves both lean on the team around the pitcher — run support, the bullpen behind him, whether his manager used him to close. They're in the score because they're in the RHOF formula, but they do the least work: where this list and Cooperstown disagree, it's usually because a pitcher's ERA and WHIP say something his win total doesn't. The HOF column marks pitchers already voted into Cooperstown.

Negro Leagues pitchers are in the pool and scored the same way — Satchel Paige, Bullet Rogan, and Willie Foster all land inside the top 50. Unlike the hitters list, there's no shortened-schedule carve-out yet, so an arm whose surviving record falls short of 1,000 innings (Smokey Joe Williams, Bill Foster) is left out for now.

Where the list and your gut disagree

Two it leaves out

Tom Glavine and Mark Buehrle both have Hall-of-Fame win totals and 3,000-plus innings, and the list leaves out both — Glavine 215th, Buehrle 750th. The score is built almost entirely from rate stats, and it rewards missing bats. Neither of them did.

#215 Tom Glavine Starter · 1987–2008 · ATL

305–203 · 3.54 ERA · 1.31 WHIP · 122 ERA+ · 74.0 bWAR

ERA+0.51
WHIP+0.24
K/9-0.53
WIN%+0.61
SV-0.38

Two Cy Youngs and 305 wins, but the profile is "good, never overpowering" — a below-average strikeout rate and a WHIP barely above the line. Winning percentage is his best category and it's the softest input in the formula. Twenty-two seasons of it, and the per-inning edge is still thin: 215th.

#750 Mark Buehrle Starter · 2000–2015 · CHW

214–160 · 3.81 ERA · 1.28 WHIP · 118 ERA+ · 60.0 bWAR

ERA+0.21
WHIP+0.24
K/9-0.94
WIN%+0.45
SV-0.35

The platonic innings-eater — 200-plus innings a year for over a decade, a perfect game and a no-hitter. But a strikeout rate a full standard deviation below his era, and ERA and WHIP only a hair better than league. Durability the score barely credits; bat-missing it prizes. It nets out just below average: 750th.

The contrast is Don Sutton, 28th — 324 wins across 23 years, never a Cy Young, never an ERA title, "just" two decades of reliably better-than-average innings that add up. A rate-based score rewards that shape when the volume is there and the rates stay above the line — and drops you fast when they don't, which is where Glavine and Buehrle land.

SAA = weighted blend of five per-season z-scores (ERA, WHIP [(H+BB)/IP], strikeout rate [SO×9/IP], winning percentage [W/(W+L)], and save count) against that season's league mean and spread among 50+ IP pitchers — weights 25 / 25 / 20 / 20 / 10, ERA and WHIP inverted so lower is a positive z — summed across every 40+ IP season of a 1,000+ career-IP pitcher, each season scaled to a 200-inning workload. Source: the Lahman database's Pitching table (2025 release, majors and Negro Major Leagues); Baseball-Reference WAR and ERA+; Hall of Fame membership current through the 2026 induction class.
